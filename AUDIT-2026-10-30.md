## 1. Method, and why the window is what it is

**Ground truth is the payout.** Candidate bases are scored against Kalshi's
`expiration_value` and `result`, never against a feed derived from Kalshi. This repo made the
circular-measurement mistake once already (the retracted 2026-08-21 TWC finding), so
observations come from the IEM ASOS archive and the CLI from the IEM CLI archive, both
independent of Kalshi. The production ledger is read only to answer "what did the live engine
actually see", never to establish truth.

**The retention cliff is real and was measured, not assumed.** Kalshi's `status=settled`
listing returns **exactly 67 days regardless of `--days`** — requesting 140 days returns the
same 402 markets per series, with the cliff at **2026-08-23**:

      KXHIGHNY   markets=402  days=67  span=2026-08-23..2026-10-28  with_expiration_value=402

So the API leg is **469 events** with the exact settled integer. The local `market_results`
ledger is not subject to that cliff and holds **700 events back to 2026-07-21**, but records
only yes/no — a two-integer range, not a value. Both legs are reported separately.

**The ledger leg is only usable if ticker geometry can be reconstructed, and that was
validated rather than assumed.** `market_results` stores no floor/cap/strike_type, so
`_db_events` recovers them from the ticker — and `T<n>` is ambiguous, being the `less` tail at
an event's bottom and the `greater` tail at its top. Getting it backwards would silently
invert every tail call. Reconstructed geometry was compared against Kalshi's own API rows on
all **469 overlapping events: 0 mismatches** on floor, cap, strike_type and result.

**No event was silently dropped.** `settlement_basis.collect` discards station-days with
fewer than 20 archive observations (a guard against an incomplete pull). Verified for this
window: that filter excluded **0 of 469** events, every event has observations, and every
event has a CLI value. One API event (`KXHIGHLAX-26OCT22`) carries no `expiration_value`, which
is why the exact denominator is 468 and the bucket denominator 469.

Final window: **2026-07-22 .. 2026-10-28, 693 settled events, 7 cities** (469 API + 224
ledger-only). Summer through late autumn; temperatures 61–102 °F. **Still no winter.**

## 3. THE HEADLINE FINDING: the disagreement axis and the risk axis are different axes

This is where the brief's framing needs adjusting, and it changes the conclusion.

Every branch of `WeatherMarket.deterministic_lock_in` proves its outcome by showing the
settlement must be **at or above** a boundary:

      between [F, F+1]   certain NO    needs  m >= F+1.5   =>  round(max) >= F+2
      less    cap C      certain NO    needs  m >= C-0.5   =>  round(max) >= C
      greater floor F    certain YES   needs  m >= F+0.5   =>  round(max) >= F+1

So a settlement that comes in **higher** than the truth leaves all three conclusions intact,
and only one that comes in **lower** breaks them. Asserted exhaustively against the production
rule in `tests/test_v43.py`: upward error harmless in 3/3 branches, downward error fatal in
3/3.

**Consequence 1 — the known counterexample is harmless.** MIA 2026-08-29 settled **+5 above**
the truth. Replaying every market in that event at the observed 85.00 °F produces **zero
detections** (the lowest tail is `T88`, needing m >= 87.5), and even had the max been higher,
every certain-NO below 90 would still have graded correct. **A $185 loss was never available
on that day.**

**Consequence 2 — "how often does Kalshi disagree with the CLI" is the wrong question.** The
right one is "how often does settlement land below what we observed", and the second rate is
**3x the first**. Answering the first does not bound the second.

**Consequence 3 — and this is the caution.** The upward direction of the single Kalshi-side
error is **not a structural guarantee** (INFERRED, stated as such). The mechanism — reading the
CLI once at or near first publication and not re-reading — is just as capable of latching a
value later revised **up** as **down**. n=1 establishes no direction. The repo's own
`kalshi-cli-revision-risk` note already says this; this audit does not weaken it.

## 4. Every event where Kalshi settled BELOW our observed max (3 of 468 = 0.64%)

      date        stn    settled  CLI    obs      R      paid
      2026-08-27  KNYC      77     77   80.00   -3.00   [None, 79]
      2026-09-20  KMDW      69     69   69.80   -0.80   [69, 70]
      2026-08-27  KMIA      93     93   93.20   -0.20   [93, 94]

**Kalshi equals the CLI exactly on all three.** None is a settlement error. All three are
failures of *our* observation, and they split into two distinct mechanisms:

### 4a. KNYC 2026-08-27, R = −3.00 — a sensor spike that NWS QC removed

      19:42Z   69.98 F
      19:51Z   80.06 F   <- +10.08 F in 9 minutes = 67.2 F/hour
      19:59Z   71.06 F   <- back down 9 F in 8 minutes

      METAR KNYC 271951Z AUTO 1/2SM +RA BKN044 ... 27/21 A3003 RMK AO2 ... T02670211 $

A 10 °F spike up and straight back down in 17 minutes, during **half-mile-visibility heavy
rain**, and the METAR carries its own **`$` maintenance indicator** — the station reporting that
it needs service. NWS QC removed it; the CLI reads 77; Kalshi settled 77.

**Our observation was wrong and the settlement was right.** This is a different risk from the
MIA case and it is the more common one.

### 4b. KMDW 2026-09-20 (−0.80) and KMIA 2026-08-27 (−0.20) — whole-°C quantization

Both maxima are exact whole degrees Celsius — **69.8 °F = 21.0 °C**, **93.2 °F = 34.0 °C** — i.e.
body-group readings carrying ±0.5 °C = **±0.9 °F** of true resolution. Both were accepted into
`daily_tmax` and both produced detections. **Both graded correct, because the 0.5 °F margin
absorbed the error — but on KMDW with no slack at all:**

      KXHIGHCHI-26SEP20-T69   YES iff T <= 68   certain-NO fires at m >= 68.5
      observed 69.80 -> fires;  settled 69 -> NO -> CORRECT
      but 68 would have settled it YES. The proof's margin was fully consumed.

Pinned in `tests/test_v43.py` so the headroom is documented rather than rediscovered.

Grading is non-circular: `SettlementReconciler.grade_lockin_events` compares the recorded side
against `market_results`, i.e. Kalshi's own payout, and refuses to overwrite a grade.

**The 31 wrong `nws5min` calls are the predicted consequence of a known defect, not a new
one.** Every one sits at a whole-°C reading (margins of exactly ±0.2/±0.4/±0.6/±0.8 °F:

documents and enforces this — the feed is wired to `observe_shadow_lockin` and "cannot reach
`_submit` even in principle."

⚠ **But read it as the warning it is: 3.34% against a 1.86% breakeven.** If that feed were
ever promoted to a trading input it would lose money *fast*. It is one routing change away.

## 7. Does "Strategy 1 is riskless" survive?

**No — but what replaces it is not "the edge is destroyed" either.** Precisely:

### What the numbers support

At the real fill size (189 contracts) and the live 98¢ ceiling, using production's own fee
model (`weather_arb.fees`):

      win  net  +$3.52        lose net  -$185.48        ratio 1 : 52.7
      breakeven wrong-rate     1.862%   =  1 bad settlement per 54 lock-ins

- The live record is **0 wrong in 940 graded detections**, bounding the wrong-rate at
  **< 0.41%** (Wilson, 95%) — comfortably **below** the 1.86% breakeven. The brief's "a 0.5%
  settlement failure rate destroys the edge" is arithmetically right, and the measured bound
  sits below it.
- So **settlement risk does not currently destroy the edge.** At 0.41% the EV is still
  +$2.79/lock-in.

### What they do not support

1. **"Riskless" is the wrong word.** Two independent mechanisms can make settlement differ
   from what we proved: Kalshi settling a withdrawn CLI value (1/468, measured) and our own
   observation being wrong (3/468, measured). Neither is zero, and the first is **invisible to
   every guard in this repo by construction**.
2. **The safety is contingent, not structural.** `CLAUDE.md` credits `R >= 0`. That is **false
   at this scale** (R < 0 on 3/468, min −3.00). What actually holds the line is the glitch
   guard (§5) and the 0.5 °F margin (§4b) — both of which were fully consumed in at least one
   observed case each. They are tuning parameters, not theorems.
3. **The sample that matters is far smaller than 940.** Only **1 of 274** detections has ever
   been tradeable. The risk is bounded by 940 detections; the *profitability* rests on a
   tradeable sample of **n = 1** (+$3.64, against a predicted +$3.73).
4. **No winter.** 98 days of summer and autumn. The CLI-vs-observation gap, the guard's rate
   constant, and the quantization margin are all unmeasured at low temperatures and in snow/ice
   regimes where sensor faults differ.

### The verdict

**Strategy 1 remains near-riskless *in the measured record* and remains economically
pointless.** This audit does not change the funding conclusion, and it does not change it in
the direction the brief anticipated: the binding constraint is still **opportunity scarcity**
— one tradeable lock-in and $3.64 in six weeks — not settlement failure. Settlement risk is
real, bounded below breakeven, and dominated by mechanisms on *our* side of the wire that the
existing guards happen to catch.

**What would change the verdict:** a single downward Kalshi-side revision. One would take the
observed basis-failure rate to 2/468 with a direction, and if it landed on a tradeable lock-in
it would erase ~50 of them. Nothing in 468 events rules it out.

## 8. Still unknown (not measured — do not assume)

1. **Whether Kalshi-side settlement errors are directionally biased.** n=1. The +5 on MIA is
   not evidence of an upward tendency, only of one upward instance.
2. **Winter.** Everything here is July–October.
3. **How often the CLI is revised at all.** `cli_revision_audit.py`'s count is a *floor* — a
   revision Kalshi caught leaves both numbers agreeing and is invisible to the method.
4. **The whole-°C fallback trigger** on routine METARs (`CROSS-VENUE-STEP1.md` §2: whitespace
   is not it). It sets the width of the §4b margin consumption and remains UNKNOWN.
5. **Whether the guard's 8 °F/hour constant is right at other stations/seasons.** It caught
   67.2 °F/hour with enormous headroom; the hard cases are the 10–20 °F/hour ones, and none
   appeared in this window.
